Heatstroke Gap: Cool Regions Faced Higher Risk Than Warm Ones

Heatstroke risk in Japan’s coolest northern regions is about twice that of warmer areas, even under the same summer heat, based on an analysis of 790,000 emergency cases from 2008–2022.

What We Found

Heatstroke risk in cool regions is about twice that in warm ones. On days when the high reached 35°C, cooler areas saw roughly 2 emergency transports per 100,000 people, compared with about 1 in warmer regions.

Why It Matters

Japan uses a single nationwide threshold for heat alerts, but risk is not the same everywhere. People who live in cooler climates are less used to extreme heat and often have homes and communities that are less prepared for it—so a sudden spike can hit harder.

The findings suggest heat alerts should be more local—a challenge not just in Japan, but also in northern Europe, Canada, and northern U.S. states, where historically cool regions now see record heat.

Visual Highlight

Cool zones (<22°C summer average) show nearly double the heatstroke incidence at the same daily temperature.

* To keep the chart responsive, only about 10% of points are shown. The smooth curves are based on the full dataset.

How We Did It

Dataset & Approach

  • Emergency transport records for 790,000 heatstroke cases (2008–2022).
  • Daily temperature and humidity from each prefectural capital.
  • Population data and a simple three-way climate grouping based on average summer temperature.
  • Tested several climate and demographic variables; summer average temperature explained regional differences best.

Validation

The choice of variables and the framing of the findings were reviewed with a climate reporter, and a university epidemiologist checked that the patterns matched what is known about how the human body adapts to heat.

Gasoline Market: Data Revealed Signs of Possible Price Coordination

An analysis of 20 years of government data found that gasoline prices in one Japanese prefecture remained unusually stable for months. Reporting then found that many local dealers had informally aligned their posted prices, raising possible competition-law concerns.

What We Found

Government data showed three unusual patterns in Kochi: gasoline prices stayed almost unchanged for months, remained well above the national average, and fell sharply after suspected price-fixing was reported in Nagano in 2025.

Interviews with industry sources then found that many stations in Kochi had kept their posted roadside prices at almost the same level under an informal understanding.

Why It Matters

Gasoline prices directly affect household budgets and have become a major public concern as global energy shocks push fuel costs higher and the Japanese government spends heavily on subsidies to contain prices.

Our reporting also found a longer history behind Kochi's unusual market. After years of intense price competition that had exhausted local dealers, cooperation among businesses gradually increased, helping explain how the market became less competitive.

Visual Highlight

Kochi's gasoline prices have become unusually high compared with the national average, especially over the past decade. The chart also shows repeated periods in recent years when prices barely moved for months, unlike the more frequent fluctuations seen elsewhere in Japan.

How We Did It

We analysed about 20 years of weekly gasoline-price data published by Japan's Agency for Natural Resources and Energy, comparing price movements across all prefectures. The analysis identified unusually long periods of almost no price movement in Kochi, as well as prices well above the national average.

These patterns led us to report in Kochi. Interviews with current and former industry figures found that many stations had kept their posted roadside prices at almost the same level under an informal understanding. We also spoke with competition-law experts to examine the possible legal implications.

My Role

  • Built the R workflow for cleaning, comparing and visualising long-term prefectural gasoline-price data.
  • Identified the unusually stable price pattern in Kochi and used it to guide the reporting.
  • Conducted interviews with industry sources and experts.
  • Reported and wrote the investigation.

Impact & Publication

The investigation was published on the front page and in the newspaper's main national news section of The Asahi Shimbun.
