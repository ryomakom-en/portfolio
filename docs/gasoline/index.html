Gasoline Market: Unusually Stable Prices Suggested Coordination

An analysis of 20 years of government data found that gasoline prices in one Japanese prefecture remained unusually stable for months; in subsequent interviews, local dealers acknowledged informally aligning their posted prices to avoid competition.

What We Found

Government data showed three unusual patterns in Kochi: gasoline prices stayed almost unchanged for months—a pattern not seen elsewhere in Japan to the same extent— remained well above the national average, and fell sharply after suspected price-fixing was reported in Nagano in 2025.

Interviews with industry sources then found that many stations in Kochi had kept their posted roadside prices at almost the same level under an informal understanding, with multiple sources acknowledging that the practice was intended to avoid price competition.

Why It Matters

Gasoline prices directly affect household budgets and have become a major public concern as global energy shocks push fuel costs higher and the Japanese government spends heavily on subsidies to contain prices.

Our reporting also found a longer history behind Kochi's unusual market. After years of intense price competition that had exhausted local dealers, cooperation among businesses gradually increased, helping explain how the market became less competitive.

Visual Highlight

Kochi's gasoline prices have become unusually high compared with the national average, especially over the past decade. The chart also shows repeated periods in recent years when prices barely moved for months, unlike the more frequent fluctuations seen elsewhere in Japan.

How We Did It

We analysed about 20 years of weekly gasoline-price data published by Japan's Agency for Natural Resources and Energy, comparing price movements across all prefectures. The analysis identified unusually long periods of almost no price movement in Kochi, as well as prices well above the national average.

These patterns led us to report in Kochi. Interviews with current and former industry figures found that many stations had kept their posted roadside prices at almost the same level under an informal understanding. We also spoke with competition-law experts to examine the possible legal implications.
