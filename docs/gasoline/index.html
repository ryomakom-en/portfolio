Gasoline Market: Data Revealed Signs of Possible Price Coordination

An analysis of 20 years of government data found that gasoline prices in one Japanese prefecture remained unusually stable for months. Reporting then found that many local dealers had informally aligned their posted prices, raising possible competition-law concerns.

What We Found

Gasoline prices in Kochi Prefecture stayed almost unchanged for long periods even while prices in other parts of Japan moved up and down with crude oil prices and market conditions.

The prefecture's average regular-gasoline price stayed in the 176-yen range from August 2022 to July 2023, and in the 179-yen range from October 2023 to July 2024. Prices were also among the highest in Japan.

Why It Matters

The unusual pattern raised a simple reporting question: why did one local market behave so differently from the rest of the country?

Interviews with industry insiders suggested that many dealers had shared an understanding about posted prices. A former competition-law investigator said that exchanging information with the understanding that posted prices would be aligned could raise concerns under Japan's Antimonopoly Act, even without an explicit agreement.

From Data to Field Reporting

The project began with a pattern in nationwide data, not with a tip about price coordination. The long period of unusually flat prices led to reporting in Kochi, where interviews with former and current industry figures helped explain what the data alone could not.

This is the kind of data journalism I value: using data to see the broader pattern, then using reporting to understand the concrete reality behind it.

How We Did It

Dataset & Approach

  • Analysed about 20 years of weekly gasoline-price data published by Japan's Agency for Natural Resources and Energy.
  • Compared price movements across all prefectures to identify periods that looked unusually stable.
  • Examined Kochi's position in the national price distribution as well as changes over time.
  • Used the data pattern to guide interviews with local industry sources and competition-law experts.

Reporting & Validation

Multiple industry sources described an informal practice of aligning the prices shown on roadside signs, while saying that some competition continued through discounts and loyalty programs. A competition-law specialist reviewed the facts and explained the possible legal implications.

My Role

  • Developed the story from the initial nationwide data analysis.
  • Built the R workflow for cleaning, comparing and visualising long-term prefectural gasoline-price data.
  • Identified the unusually stable price pattern in Kochi and used it to guide the reporting.
  • Conducted interviews with industry sources and experts.
  • Reported and wrote the investigation.

Impact & Publication

  • The investigation showed how a nationwide public dataset could reveal an unusual local market pattern that was difficult to see through individual price observations alone.
  • After concerns about price coordination grew in the industry, the local association urged members to comply with competition law, and prices later fell sharply.
